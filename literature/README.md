## Why these methods fit this problem

The decisions are categorical and strongly coupled: moving one two-period lab can affect several occupied slots, and assigning a teacher changes every event in that offering. Continuous gradient methods cannot directly enforce the categorical choices or no-clash logic. A constructive heuristic is useful because it exposes feasibility and provides a fast, interpretable reference. Exact constraint programming is useful for small-instance verification and explicit infeasibility/optimality statuses. A Pareto population method is useful when the feasible objective vectors contain multiple trade-offs and no coordinator-provided scalar weights exist.

NSGA-II supplies a transparent comparator with familiar selection and survival mechanisms. Its generic crossover can destroy feasible schedules, which motivates testing intelligent initialization, repair, or local improvement. That enhancement is justified by a specific limitation and must be measured against standard NSGA-II under a declared budget. A larger catalog of NSGA-III, MOEA/D, SA, or weighted-sum methods would add code and tuning effort; their names alone would not strengthen the academic result.

## Evaluation cautions from the problem structure

An excellent greedy schedule can dominate many evolutionary candidates. A larger archive can contain duplicate objective vectors rather than new trade-offs. A front with more points is not automatically better. Workload imbalance may be constant when teacher choices are fixed. The relative importance of gaps and room capacity is a policy issue that an indicator cannot settle. These are reasons to report feasibility, objective ranges, distinct front vectors, runtime, convergence, and representative timetables together.

The project uses synthetic instances with explicit assumptions rather than importing an International Timetabling Competition dataset into a different model and claiming direct benchmark comparability. Real-data and standard-benchmark validation are future work requiring careful mapping of cohorts, rooms, faculty, constraints, and objective definitions.


## Implemented algorithm details and accounting

The standard comparator starts from independently sampled categorical domains, without constructive seeding, repair, or local search. Whole-offering uniform crossover preserves one teacher gene per offering, while mutations may change times, rooms, event positions, or teachers. Deb-style constrained ranking, normalized crowding, binary tournaments, and elitist survival drive selection. Only feasible discovered vectors enter its archive; equal objective vectors do not become extra trade-offs. Domain-valid categorical initialization does not imply joint feasibility: resource clashes, availability interactions, and teaching limits still need evaluation.

The enhanced configuration can enable intelligent initialization, reconstruction repair, and short local search independently for ablations. Repair reconstructs offering placements with preference penalties for changing inherited genes. It is neither a Kempe-chain implementation nor a guarantee of minimum timetable disruption. Local proposals sample compaction, better-fit rooms, load-aware teacher changes, or cross-qualified whole-offering teacher exchanges and are accepted only when feasible and improving a sampled positive scalar direction. Pareto archiving still uses the original three objectives, not that local scalar.

Every full call to the common objective/constraint evaluator, including seeding, repair results, and local-search candidates, counts against the recorded candidate-evaluation budget. Partial feasibility checks inside constructive scheduling do not count as full objective evaluations, but their cost is included in wall runtime. Consequently equal evaluation counts do not imply equal compute. Show both counts and runtime, and describe a repeated constructive baseline honestly if it receives the same number of complete candidate evaluations as NSGA-II.


## When a global statement is justified

A discovered archive alone does not certify global optimality, but an attaining lower-bound certificate can. The post-study small-instance analysis relaxes eligibility/timing while exactly optimizing indivisible offering-load partitions and independently lower-bounds room wastage and gaps. Saved feasible schedules attain all three bounds on the three executed small cases, certifying a singleton global ideal vector. This does not contradict the conflicting exact micro-examples: objective conflict is instance-dependent. See formulation Section 5.1 and the saved ideal certificates; no corresponding global claim is made for medium or large cases.
